Extraction of iron
Step 1 Hot air is blasted near the bottom of the furnace. Coke (carbon)
will react with oxygen in air
Carbon + Oxygen ➔ Carbon dioxide
C + O2 ➔ CO2
Step 2 Coke will further react with the carbon dioxide produced in step 1
to form carbon monoxide
Carbon + Carbon dioxide ➔ Carbon monoxide
C + CO2 ➔ 2CO
Step 3 Carbon monoxide will react with haematite (Fe2O3). Iron metal
can be obtained in this step. Fe2O3 is reduced to Fe in this step.
The reducing agent used is carbon monoxide.
Carbon monoxide + Iron(III) oxide ➔ Carbon dioxide + Iron
3CO + Fe2O3 ➔ 3CO2 + 2Fe
- Limestone is also added to the blast furnace. It is decomposed upon heating to form calcium oxide and carbon dioxide.
Calcium oxide can react with the impurities such as silicon dioxide to form slag and it is further removed.
- Calcium carbonate ➔ Calcium oxide + Carbon dioxide
CaCO3 ➔ CaO + CO2
- Calcium oxide + Silicon dioxide ➔ Calcium silicate
CaO + SiO2 ➔ CaSiO3

Extraction of aluminium
Raw materials required:
1. Bauxite (Source of aluminium ore, Al2O3)
2. Cryolite (To reduce the melting point of aluminium ore)
- At negative electrode: Aluminum ion + electrons ➔ Aluminum
Al3+ + 3e- ➔ Al
At positive electrode: Oxide ion ➔ Oxygen + electrons
2O2- ➔ O2 + 4e-
- Aluminium oxide must be in molten form. The melting point of bauxite is very high (2000 ℃). Therefore the bauxite is
dissolved in molten cyolite (a less common aluminum ore) so that the melting point of the mixture of aluminium ore will be
reduced to below 2000 ℃.
Relation between metal reactivity series and the extraction method
- Reactive metals (element) form stable ore (compound)
➔ Lower the position of the metal in the reactivity series, the more easily it can be extracted from ore.
Q: Explain why iron was discovered and used much earlier than aluminium
A: Iron is less reactive than aluminium. Ore of irons are less stable. Therefore iron can be extracted more easily.
Factors affecting availability of metals
1. Abundance of the metal in Earth’s crust
2. Ease and cost of mining the ore
3. Ease and cost of extracting the metal from its ore
In general, the more easily a metal can be extracted, the earlier it was discovered in history.
Ways of conserving metals
1. Reusing metal articles
2. Reducing the use of metals
3. Recycling used metals

Displacement reaction
- A metal will displace a less reactive metal from solution of the compound of the less reactive metal, but not vice versa.
Example 1: Zinc + Copper(II) sulphate ➔ Copper + Zinc sulphate
Zinc is more reactive than copper. Zinc displaces copper metal from the copper(II) sulphate solution.
Example 2: Silver + Zinc sulphate ➔ No reaction
Silver is less reactive than zinc. There is no reaction between silver and zinc sulphate solution
Note: When potassium / sodium / calcium is added to the solution of a metal compound, colorless gas will also be given
out because these metals can react with water in the solution directly.

Steps for writing an ionic equation
1. Write down the FULL chemical equation
3 H2SO4(aq) + 2 Al(OH)3(s) → Al2(SO4)3(aq) + 6 H2O(l)
Note: This is the reaction between acid and a base, producing salt and water
2. Split the compounds that are soluble in water (aqueous state) into individual ions
6 H+(aq) + 3 SO42-(aq) + 2 Al(OH)3(s) → 2 Al3+(aq) + 3 SO42-(aq) + 6 H2O(l)
Note: Pay attention to the coefficient of H+. Do not write 3H2. It is an acid not hydrogen gas.
Al(OH)3 is insoluble in water. Do NOT split it into Al3+ and 3OH-
3. Cancel out the ions that appear in both sides. Simplify the equation if needed.
6 H+(aq) + 3 SO42-(aq) + 2 Al(OH)3(s) → 2 Al3+(aq) + 3 SO42-(aq) + 6 H2O(l)
6 H+(aq) + 2 Al(OH)3(s) → 2 Al3+(aq) + 6 H2O(l)
Ionic equation: 3 H+(aq) + Al(OH)3(s) → Al3+(aq) + 3 H2O(l)
Note: Since SO42- does not participate in the reaction, we call them ‘spectator ion’.
4. If ALL ions are cancelled out, there is NO REACTION between 2 compounds
NaCl(aq) + KBr(aq) → NaBr(aq) + KCl(aq)
Na+(aq) + Cl-(aq) + K+(aq) + Br-(aq) → Na+(aq) + Br-(aq) + K+(aq) + Cl-(aq)

Mole calculation
Mole = Mass
Molar mass (Mole) × (Avogadro′s constant) = No. of formula unit
1. Calculate the number of oxygen atoms in 60.1 g of quartz. (Relative atomic masses: O = 16.0, Si = 28.1)
Answer: Mole of SiO2 = (mass) / (molar mass) = 60.1 / (28.1 + 16x2) = 1
Number of formula unit (i.e. amount of SiO2) = (mole) x (Avogadro’s constant)
= 1 x 6.02 x 1023 = 6.02 x 1023
Since 1 SiO2 contains 2 O atoms, number of atoms = 6.02 x 1023 x 2 = 1.204 x 1024
2. Calculate the number of atoms in 14.0 g of nitrogen gas. (Relative atomic mass of N = 14.0)
Answer: Mole of nitrogen (N2) = mass / molar mass = 14 / 14x2 = 0.5
Number of formula unit (i.e. amount of N2 molecules) = (mole) x (Avogadro’s constant)
= 0.5 x (6.02 x 1023) = 3.01 x 1023
Since 1 N2 molecules contains 2 N atoms, number of atoms = 3.01 x 1023 x 2 = 6.02 x 1023
3. Calculate the number of ions in 1 mol of iron(III) sulphate
Answer: Number of formula unit (i.e. amount of Fe2(SO4)3) = (mole) x (Avogadro’s constant)
= 1 x (6.02 x 1023) = 6.02 x 1023
Since 1 Fe2(SO4)3 contains 2 Fe3+ ions and 3 SO42- ions, number of ions = 6.02 x 1023 x (2+3) = 3.01 x 1024 ions
4. Calculate the mass of 30624700 C atoms (Relative atomic mass of C = 12.0)
Answer: Number of moles of 30624700 C atoms = (no. of formula unit) / (Avogadro’s constant)
= 30624700 / 6.02 x 1023 = 5.087 x 10-17
Mass of 30624700 C atoms = (mole) x (Molar mass) = 5.087 x 10-17 x 12.0 = 6.10 x 10-16 g.
5. Calculate the mass of 534202 oxygen molecules. (Relative atomic mass of O = 16.0)
Answer: Number of moles of 534202 oxygen molecules (O2) = (no. of formula unit) / (Avogadro’s constant)
= 534202 / 6.02 x 1023 = 8.87 x 10-19
Mass of 534202 oxygen molecules = (mole) x (Molar mass) = 8.87 x 10-19 x (16.0 x 2) = 2.84 x 10-17 g.

Mole calculation and percentage yield
1. In an experiment, 17 g of NH3 and excess O2 are allowed to react. The equation for the reaction is shown below:
4NH3 + 5O2 ➔ 4NO + 6H2O
(Relative atomic masses: H = 1.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0)
(a) Calculate the theoretical yield of H2O.
Answer: Mole of NH3 used = 17 / (14 + 1x3) = 1
Theoretical mole of H2O obtained = 1 x 6/4 = 1.5
Theoretical mass of H2O obtained = 1.5 x (1x2 + 16) = 27 g
(b) If the mass of H2O obtained is 5.4 g, calculate the percentage yield of H2O
Answer: Percentage yield = Actual mass / theoretical mass x 100% = 5.4/27 x 100% = 20%
(c) Suggest a reason why the percentage yield cannot reach 100%.
Answer: Incomplete reaction
2. Under certain conditions, 1.0 g of SiO2 is allowed to react with 1.0 g of Mg. The equation for the reaction is shown below:
SiO2 + 2Mg ➔ 2MgO + Si

Corrosion
- Corrosion of metal refers to the gradual deterioration of a metal, resulting from the reaction of the metal with air, water or
other substances in the environment.
In general, the more reactive a metal, the more rapidly it corrodes.
Note: Rusting refers to the corrosion of iron only, but not other metals
Chemistry of rusting
Essential conditions of rusting: Water and oxygen
Step 1: Iron ➔ Iron(II) ion (Fe ➔ Fe2+ + 2e-)
Water + Oxygen ➔ Hydroxide ion (O2 + 2H2O + 4e- ➔ 4OH-)
Step 2: Iron(II) ion + Hydroxide ion ➔ Iron(II) hydroxide (Fe2+ + 2OH- ➔ Fe(OH)2)
Step 3: Iron(II) hydroxide + Water + Oxygen ➔ Iron(III) hydroxide (4Fe(OH)2 + O2 + 2H2O ➔ 4Fe(OH)3)
Step 4: Iron(III) hydroxide ➔ Hydrated iron(III) oxide (Rust, reddish brown solid)
Overall equation: 4Fe(s) + 3O2(g) + 2nH2O(l) ➔ 2Fe2O3⸱nH2O
Experiments related to rusting
Tube 1: No rusting will occur. Anhydrous calcium chloride is a drying agent. It removes water from the air. Therefore the iron
nail in tube 1 has no contact with water.
Tube 2: No rusting will occur. Boiled distilled water removes dissolved oxygen in water. The oil layer prevents oxygen from
dissolving in water again. Therefore the iron nail in tube 2 has no contact with oxygen.
Tube 3: Rusting will occur. It is because iron nail in tube 3 has contact with water and oxygen. Note that normal distilled water
still contains dissolved oxygen.
Blue area: When iron rusts, iron(II) ion (Fe2+) will be formed. It turns potassium hexacyanoferrate(III) (K3Fe(CN)6) blue.
Pink area: Hydroxide ion (OH-) forms during rusting. It turns phenolphthalein pink.
Note 1: Uneven, scratched, bent or sharp area of an iron object will rust more readily.
Note 2: Gel can slow down the diffusion of the color patches for easier observation

Factors that speed up rusting
1. Presence of salt (Salt increases the electrical conductivity)
2. Presence of acid (Acid increases the electrical conductivity and promotes iron(II) ion formation)
3. High temperature (Higher temperature, higher reaction rate)
4. Attachment of less reactive metal (e.g. tin, lead, copper, silver) to iron
Corrosion resistance of aluminium
Aluminium has a thin layer of aluminium oxide which is impermeable to water and oxygen.
Anodization: A process to thicken the aluminium oxide layer on the surface of aluminium.
In anodization, the aluminium object is connected to a positive terminal of the battery
Anodized aluminium object has a higher corrosion resistance and can be dyed more easily.
